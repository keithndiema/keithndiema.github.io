Limit Order Book

C++17, Python, SQL, R

Tested and benchmarked

A matching engine with strict price-time priority, plus an agent-based market simulator and an Avellaneda-Stoikov market-making strategy. The core is a dense price ladder with an occupancy bitmap and intrusive FIFO queues, exposed to Python through pybind11.

Measured: 5.9 to 10.5 million operations per second, with limit-order latency of 28 to 36 ns at the median, on one virtualised 2.1 GHz core. That is 4.4 to 23 times a std::map baseline. The 23 times is that baseline's worst case, and through the Python API the speed-up is only about 1.6 times.

  • 42 C++ tests, including about 1.1 million fuzzed operations checked against a reference book, repeated under AddressSanitizer and UBSan.
  • Mutation testing: six known bugs are injected and every one must make a test fail.
  • Price impact of order-flow imbalance computed in SQL, NumPy and R. All three agree at 0.0304 ticks per lot.
The market-making maths

Avellaneda-Stoikov quotes around a reservation price that leans against inventory $q$, with risk aversion $\gamma$ and volatility $\sigma$:

$$r = s - q\,\gamma\,\sigma^{2}(T-t), \qquad \delta^{a}+\delta^{b} = \gamma\,\sigma^{2}(T-t) + \frac{2}{\gamma}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{k}\right)$$

Vestora

Python, FastAPI, React, C++17, R, SQL

Early, NSE first

Market intelligence for African capital markets. Trading apps help people place orders; Vestora is meant to help them understand what they are buying. Phase one covers the Nairobi Securities Exchange, and the Uganda Securities Exchange is planned next.

  • A C++17 numerical core for indicators, drawdown, Monte-Carlo VaR and CVaR, and Black-Scholes pricing, with a NumPy fallback when it isn't compiled.
  • R for the statistical tests (Ljung-Box, ARCH, Jarque-Bera, ARIMA) and SQL window-function views for analytics.
  • XGBoost forecasters, and an Isolation Forest for anomalies tuned for thin, low-liquidity markets.
  • Walk-forward evaluation with tests that guard against data leakage, so a model is only judged on data it could not have seen.

Not yet: USSD and SMS access are in development and no accuracy results are published. I'd rather say so than invent a number.

The training objective

Parameters $\theta$ are fitted by regularised least squares, with $\lambda$ chosen only on the training window of each walk-forward fold:

$$J(\theta) = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(y_t - f(x_t;\theta)\bigr)^2 + \lambda\,\lVert\theta\rVert_2^{2}$$

AgriGuard

Python, XGBoost, Prophet, FastAPI, Streamlit, USSD

Working demo, honest metrics

Crop prices and forecasts for smallholder farmers in Uganda, reachable from a smartphone app or from any phone through USSD. It uses WFP price data for about 40 markets and 38 commodities since 2006, blended with FEWS NET where they overlap.

Measured: on the latest 20% of months held out, XGBoost scores a MAE of about 544 UGX/kg and a MAPE of about 12.8%. A naive "same as last month" forecast scores almost the same. I removed earlier, better-looking figures because I couldn't reproduce them.

So the value isn't a sharper point forecast. It's calibrated uncertainty, cross-market comparison (where a crop is cheapest and dearest) and delivery to people who only have a feature phone, for example dialling 3*Maize for next month's outlook.

The Prophet decomposition

Prophet, which AgriGuard uses as a reference for confidence intervals, splits a series into trend $g$, seasonality $s$ and holiday effects $h$:

$$y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + \epsilon_t$$

Traffic Sentinel

Python, YOLOv8, OpenCV, FastAPI, Streamlit

Prototype, submitted to the Ministry of ICT

Road-safety analysis for Uganda's urban junctions. It watches traffic footage, counts cars, trucks, buses and boda-bodas, and gives each junction a risk score from 0 to 100 (low, medium, high or critical), with an hourly view of when it is most dangerous.

  • The score is a transparent weighted sum: traffic density 55%, time of day 25% and congestion frequency 20%. It is a rule a traffic officer can inspect, not a black box.
  • Uganda road-accident records are overlaid on junction coordinates, so the footage score can be read against real incidents.
  • The README reports about 6 ms per frame for YOLOv8n detection on a CPU.

FPV Sim

C++17, Python, NumPy, pygame

Playable

A first-person drone simulator with a C++ physics core (six degrees of freedom, RK4 integration at 2 kHz, a PID flight controller) and a renderer written from scratch in Python. You choose a town in Uganda, from Kampala to Mbarara, Kasese or Moroto, and race through its streets at morning, midday, golden hour or dusk.

The towns are generated from a profile of each place (coordinates, elevation, rivers, lakes, mountains and landmarks), not traced from maps. An optional mode can fetch real OpenStreetMap data and falls back to the generated town if it can't. The regression suite has 28 tests, including an autopilot that must finish a lap in several towns.

How I work

Three habits that show up in every project above.

Test the forecast, not the story

Every model is compared with a naive baseline and evaluated walk-forward, so it never sees the future. If the baseline wins, I report that.

Check one thing two ways

Fuzzing against a reference implementation, mutation testing, and the same statistic computed in three languages. Agreement across independent paths is the evidence.

Design for the connection people have

A farmer on a feature phone and a trader on fibre both get an answer. That's why AgriGuard speaks USSD and Vestora keeps SMS on its roadmap.
